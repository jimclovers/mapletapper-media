Myth or fact: it's getting too warm for Midwest sugar maples. 🍁🌡️

Partly true. Here's what the research says:

✅ True: the sap season is starting earlier at the southern edge of maple country. A Cincinnati Parks naturalist says their season used to run from February to mid-March. Now it's late December or January to mid-February.

❌ Not true (yet): sugar maples aren't leaving the Midwest. They're still common across most of the eastern US.

🔭 Looking ahead: if emissions stay high, a 2019 study projects that by 2100 the season comes about a month earlier, sap gets a little less sweet, and the best sap-flow zone moves about 250 miles north, from near Madison and Milwaukee to near Thunder Bay and International Falls.

🤔 Not everyone agrees: Quebec government scientists found syrup yields hold steady across a wide range of climates, and the US Forest Service projects only a modest decline in sugar maple habitat.

What it means for your backyard:
• Tap by the forecast, not the calendar: start when thawing days follow freezing nights
• Red and silver maples make syrup too
• Keep your trees healthy. Drought and other stress hit hardest.

Sources: Rapp et al. 2019 (Forest Ecology and Management); Houle and Duchesne (Quebec Ministry of Forests); USDA Forest Service Climate Change Tree Atlas; Ohio State University; Cincinnati Parks.
