TREE ID #3: Sugar maple, the syrup tree 🍁

This is the one sugarmakers look for. Its sap is the sweetest of any native maple (tied with its close cousin, the black maple), averaging about 2 to 2.5% sugar, and some trees run 3% or more.

How to spot it:
🍁 Usually 5 lobes with U-shaped valleys and smooth edges
🌿 Dark green on top, lighter green underneath
🔥 Vivid red, orange or yellow in fall
🌳 Gray bark with deep furrows on older trees

📸 Every photo in this post is a real sugar maple.

About 40 gallons of sap boil down to 1 gallon of syrup. Red maple takes about 50, and silver maple about 57. (Sugarmaker's Rule of 86: divide 86 by the sap's sugar % to get gallons of sap per gallon of syrup.) Found one at least 12 inches across, measured 4½ feet up? Mark it now so you can find it again in March.

Fun facts: it's the state tree of New York, Vermont, West Virginia and Wisconsin. And Canada's oldest known sugar maple, the Comfort Maple in Pelham, Ontario, is estimated at 400 to 500 years old. It was already growing when the Pilgrims landed, and today it's patched with bricks and held up by cables.
