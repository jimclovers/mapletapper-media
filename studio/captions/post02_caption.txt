MYTH OR FACT? "Tapping a maple hurts the tree." 🌳

MYTH! A taphole is a small wound, and a healthy tree handles it well. Done right, tapping takes about 10% or less of a tree's stored sugar, and only for a few weeks. Pull the spile at the end of the season and new wood starts growing over the hole; a healthy tree usually closes it within 1 to 3 years. Some maple farms have tapped the same trees for over 100 years.

The 4 tree-friendly rules:
1️⃣ Only tap trees at least 12 inches across, measured 4½ feet up
2️⃣ Never more than 2 taps on a tree (12–18": 1 tap · over 18" and healthy: up to 2)
3️⃣ Drill just 1½ inches deep: sapwood only, never the heartwood
4️⃣ Move to a new spot each year: a few inches to the side and about 12" above or below an old taphole

Why we use 5/16" "tree saver" spiles: a smaller hole does less damage and closes up sooner, and University of Vermont tests found it still gives nearly as much sap.
