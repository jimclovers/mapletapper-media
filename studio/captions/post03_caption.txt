Why maple tapping is the perfect family project 🍁

It's one of the few backyard projects where everyone, from little kids to grandparents, gets a real job. Here are 3 reasons it works so well for families:

1️⃣ There's a job for every age. Kids can pick and mark the tree, help set the spile, check the jugs each day and measure the sap. Grown-ups drill the taphole and run the boil. As our guide puts it, kids can help with every step except moving the boiling sap.

2️⃣ No farm needed. A drill with a 5/16" bit, spiles and tubing, clean jugs, a big pot, a thermometer and a felt filter will get you started. Plan to boil outdoors: each gallon of syrup sends about 39 gallons of water up as steam.

3️⃣ It's hard to mess up. Syrup is done at 7°F above the boiling point of water. Boiled it a little too thick? Stir in a splash of sap. And if you keep heating, that same syrup becomes the start of maple treats: maple cream at about 22–24°F above boiling, hard maple candy at 28–30°F, and maple sugar at 40–45°F.

🔥 Safety first: grown-ups run the boil and stay with the pot. Candy and sugar get much hotter than boiling water.
