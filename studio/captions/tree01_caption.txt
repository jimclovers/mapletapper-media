TREE ID #1: Silver maple 🍁

Can you tap it? Yes, but it's not the sweetest maple around.

How to spot it:
🍃 Much pointier leaves: 5 deep-cut lobes with toothed edges
🤍 A silvery-white underside (flip a leaf over)
💛 Fall color that tends to yellow
🌊 Low, wet ground is its favorite spot

The catch for syrup makers: silver maple sap averages about 1.5% sugar, a bit below red maple (1.5 to 2%) and well below sugar maple (about 2 to 2.5%). That works out to about 57 gallons of sap for each gallon of syrup, versus about 40 from a sugar maple. It's also one of the first trees to bud out in spring, which can cut its season short. (These are typical averages. Sap sugar varies from tree to tree and place to place.)

Fun facts: silver maple grows the biggest "helicopters" of any native maple, and its seeds can sprout right away, with no winter wait. And its Latin name, Acer saccharinum, means "sugary," just two letters off from sugar maple's Acer saccharum. Yet it's the least sweet of the three common tapping maples!
