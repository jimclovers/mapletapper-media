=== MON NOV 16 · 10am · Tree ID #4: Black maple ===
TREE ID #4: Black maple, sugar maple's twin 🍁🌳

Black maple looks so much like sugar maple that most people walk right past it. Botanists even argue over whether it's its own tree or a kind of sugar maple, and the two often cross.

How to spot it:
🌱 Tiny leafy "ears" (stipules) where the leaf stem meets the twig. Sugar maple never has them.
🍃 Leaf edges that droop, so the leaf looks a little wilted
🧤 A fuzzy, velvety underside, not just hairs along the veins
💧 It likes rich, moist ground, even near rivers

Can you tap it? Yes! Ohio State and University of Missouri tree experts say black maple sap is about as sweet as sugar maple sap. Many sugarmakers tap both side by side without ever knowing which is which.

Same rules as always: at least 12 inches across at 4½ feet up, a 5/16" tree-saver spout and food-grade gear.

Tip: the ears can fall off by late summer, so check a few leaves. Spring and summer are the easiest time.
