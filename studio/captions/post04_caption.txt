KIDS CORNER: A taphole is about the size of a pencil eraser ✏️🍁

Grab a pencil and look at the eraser end. A taphole is just a little wider!

📏 Up close: our tree-saver drill bit is 5/16 of an inch across, about 8 mm. A regular pencil is about 7 mm. A grown-up drills about 1½ inches deep, into the sapwood: the living wood just under the bark that carries sap.

🧮 Sap math: one little hole can give about 10 gallons of sap in a season. Boil that down and you get about 1 quart of syrup. On a really good sap day, one tap can drip up to about a gallon!

🌳 And the tree is fine. A maple stores plenty of sugar, and one tap takes about 10% or less. A healthy tree grows new wood over the hole in 1 to 3 years, and next year's hole goes in a fresh spot.
