Maples are lighting up the horizon right now 🍁

Fall is scouting season. You can spot maples from a long way off: look for crowns of bright red, orange and gold on hillsides, along fencerows and in yards. Then walk up and check the leaf, because red oaks, sumac and burning bush turn red too.

Why are there so many maples around town?
🏡 1800s: farm families planted sugar maples by the house and along country roads, for shade and syrup.
🌳 Fast shade: towns planted lots of quick-growing silver maples along their streets.
🍂 1930s on: Dutch elm disease killed millions of elm street trees, and many towns replanted with maples.
🔥 Today: Freeman maples, a cross of red and silver maple, line many Chicago-area parkways for their red-orange fall color.

Fun fact: an old New England custom had newlyweds plant two sugar maples by the front walk of their new home, one on each side. Some of those "wedding trees" still shade old farmhouses today.

Found a big one? Mark it now with a ribbon or a GPS pin, so you can find it again in late winter. And ask before tapping a tree that isn't yours: in many towns, parkway trees belong to the village.
