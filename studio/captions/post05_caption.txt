Which maples should you skip? 🍁 (A missing twig is fine!)

Our first post said to skip trees with broken branches. Let's be clearer: every big maple has a few dings. We mean real damage.

✅ Still tap-ready:
• A few lost twigs or small branches
• Rough, deeply furrowed bark (normal on older maples)
• An old scar that has grown over. Just tap clean wood away from it.

🌩️ Storm damage? Look up at the crown, the leafy top of the tree:
• Under 10% of it gone: tap as usual
• 10 to 25% gone: tap lightly, with just one small tap
• Over 25% gone: skip it for now. Some trees need a few years to recover.

❌ Skip trees with:
• Big dead limbs still on the trunk
• Rot: cavities, soft wood or hollow branch stubs
• Mushrooms or fungus on the trunk or roots
• Long cracks or bark peeling away
• A thin crown, or leaves stripped by insects or drought last summer

Why? A taphole is a small wound. A healthy maple walls it off and grows over it in 1 to 3 years. A hurt tree needs its stored sugar to heal, and drilling near rot can make it worse.

Storm guidelines from University of New Hampshire and Michigan State University Extension.
