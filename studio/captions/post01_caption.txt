Next spring's maple syrup starts with a fall walk. 🍁

Finding syrup trees is a perfect first job for the whole family. While the leaves are still on, maples are easy to spot. Once they drop, maples get much harder to tell apart. So this week, grab the kids and go find yours:

🍂 Check the leaf. Sugar maples usually have 5 lobes, smooth edges and rounded, U-shaped valleys. Soft maples (red and silver) have toothed edges and pointier lobes.
🌳 Read the bark. Older sugar maples are gray and deeply furrowed. Older soft maples look more layered and flaky.
🚁 Look for "helicopters." Every maple grows its winged seeds in pairs.

Found one at least 12 inches across (measured 4½ feet up) with a full canopy of leaves? Tie on a bright ribbon, plant a flag, or drop a GPS pin so you can find it again when the sap starts running. Kids can press a leaf or make a leaf rubbing to remember "their" tree.

The plan from here: mark your trees this fall, put a tapping kit on the Christmas list, and tap together in late winter. No farm or forest needed: a tree in your own yard is perfect, and just 4 taps can make about a gallon of syrup a season!
