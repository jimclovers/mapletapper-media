TREE ID #2: Red maple 🍁

Can you tap it? Yes, with a few trade-offs.

How to spot it:
🍃 Usually 3 lobes (sometimes 5) with toothed edges
🌿 Dark red twigs at the branch tips
❤️ Scarlet leaves in fall (sometimes orange or yellow)
💧 Grows everywhere from low, wet spots to higher ground

For syrup makers: red maple sap usually runs 1.5 to 2% sugar, versus about 2 to 2.5% for sugar maple. That means more sap, fuel and time for every jar. It also buds out earlier than sugar maple, which can end its season sooner. The good news: you can make great syrup from red maple, and University of Vermont researchers found its flavor indistinguishable from sugar maple syrup.

Fun facts: red maple is red in every season, with red flowers on bare branches in late winter, red leaf stems in summer, scarlet leaves in fall and red twigs all winter. Its sap still runs clear as water. It's also Rhode Island's state tree.
